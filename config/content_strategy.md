# Central Strength Gym — Content Strategy & Editorial Calendar

## Brand Voice
Supportive, expert, community-focused. We speak like a knowledgeable training partner — not a salesperson. Every post is evidence-based, actionable, and accessible to lifters from beginner to advanced. Content is written by Hao Cheng, NASM CPT.

## Topic Distribution Plan

All content is organized across 5 core categories. The target distribution ensures variety and covers the full scope of what a powerlifting gym audience cares about.

| Category | Target % | Posts per 20 | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| Powerlifting Programs | 25% | 5 | Programming, periodization, sample programs with specific sets/reps/percentages, competition prep, peaking |
| Nutrition Myths | 20% | 4 | Debunking common misconceptions with research, protein myths, fad diets, "detox" claims, meal timing myths |
| Supplements | 20% | 4 | Evidence-based supplement reviews with dosages, mechanisms, and practical guidance |
| Cardio + Strength | 15% | 3 | Concurrent training, conditioning for powerlifters, LISS vs HIIT, cardiovascular health for strength athletes |
| Rehab Movements | 20% | 4 | Prehab, injury prevention, training around pain, mobility work, corrective exercises with scientific support |

### Category Rationale
- **Powerlifting Programs** is the core identity of the gym and drives the most saves/shares
- **Nutrition Myths** generates high engagement through "myth-busting" format and comment debate
- **Supplements** provides practical value and is highly searchable
- **Cardio + Strength** is a major gap in existing content (0 posts out of 21) — this audience needs permission and guidance to do cardio
- **Rehab Movements** is nearly absent (1 post out of 21) — injury prevention/management is critical for retention and trust

## Quality Guidelines

### Scientific Rigor

**Citation grounding constraint** (added 2026-04-12 after the citation hallucination crisis):

The REFERENCES block must contain ONLY citations that the scraped source article carries in `structured_content.doi` / `structured_content.pmid`, OR inline DOI/PMID strings in the article body that have been verified against Crossref/PubMed at extraction time. **Do NOT generate citations from training memory.** A post with 1 honest citation is better than a post with 5 fabricated ones. A post with 0 citations is allowed if the source article is practitioner-voiced rather than research-citing — mark the REFERENCES block as `[INSUFFICIENT_SOURCE_DATA]` and let the grounded drafter drop the post via the `Posts/.needs_research/` signal path.

The previous "minimum 2 references" rule has been retracted because it created the hallucination pressure that led to the 2026-04-12 audit finding (38% of citations across 31 posts were hallucinated, misattributed, or unverifiable). See `audits/drafter_root_cause_2026-04-12.md` for the full root-cause analysis and `audits/grounded_drafter_design.md` for the replacement workflow.

Other rigor rules (unchanged):
- Cite specific study findings with numbers (effect sizes, percentages, doses) wherever possible
- Use DOI links or PMIDs for all journal references
- Avoid citing only blog posts or YouTube videos as primary evidence — always link to the underlying research
- When a claim is controversial, present both sides and indicate the weight of evidence

### Brand Voice Consistency
- Tone: Authoritative but approachable. Like a knowledgeable training partner, not a professor or salesperson.
- Use "you/your" language — speak directly to the reader
- Open with a bold claim or myth to hook attention
- Structure with numbered lists and bold headers for scannability
- End every post with a practical takeaway section
- Include one CTA from the approved list (rotate between: "Book your intro session at centralstrengthgyms.com", "Drop in for a free class — link in bio", "Tag a training partner who needs to see this", "Save this for your next training session")
- Always include the core hashtag set: #CentralStrength #SantaClaraFitness #CentralStrengthGym #BayAreaStrength
- Add 8-10 topic-specific hashtags per post

## Engagement Optimization Notes

### Best Performing Content Patterns (from existing posts)
1. **Myth-busting posts** generate the highest engagement — lead with a common misconception, then correct it with evidence (see post10, post03)
2. **Full program posts** drive the most saves and shares — lifters screenshot and follow these (see post13, post19)
3. **Numbered list format** (5 mistakes, 7 exercises, etc.) outperforms long-form paragraphs for carousel/feed content
4. **Specific numbers** (5g creatine, 3-6mg/kg caffeine, 0.7-1.0g/lb protein) are more memorable and shareable than vague advice
5. **"Sample workout" sections** at the end of technique posts add practical value and increase saves

### Posting Strategy
- **Frequency**: 3-4 posts per week
- **Mix**: Alternate between categories to maintain variety — never post the same category back-to-back
- **Timing**: Post during peak engagement windows (6-8 AM, 12-1 PM, 6-8 PM Pacific)
- **Carousel vs Single**: Myth-busting and program posts work best as carousels (multiple slides). Supplement and nutrition posts work well as single images with long captions.

### Hashtag Strategy
- Always include 4 core branded hashtags
- Add 8-10 topic-specific hashtags
- Mix high-volume tags (#StrengthTraining, #Powerlifting) with niche tags (#RPETraining, #EvidenceBasedFitness)
- Rotate hashtags to avoid shadowban risk — don't use identical hashtag sets on consecutive posts

### Content Sequencing
- Follow a "high engagement" post (myth-busting, controversy) with a "high value" post (program, practical guide)
- Pair supplement posts with the related training or nutrition post (e.g., beta-alanine after a hypertrophy program post)
- Use rehab/prehab posts after heavy training content to show the gym cares about longevity, not just PRs
