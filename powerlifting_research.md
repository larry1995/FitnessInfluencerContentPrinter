## TOPIC 1: POWERLIFTING PROGRAMS

### Overview

Powerlifting programs can be broadly categorized by experience level:
- **Beginner (Novice):** Linear Progression (LP) — add weight every session
- **Intermediate:** Weekly or block periodization — add weight every week or every training block
- **Advanced:** Complex periodization — longer cycles, more variation needed

---

### Program 1: Starting Strength (Mark Rippetoe)

**Best For:** Absolute beginners (0–6 months of training)

**Structure:**
- 3 days/week, full-body each session (alternating Workout A and B)
- Core lifts: Squat (every session), Bench Press, Deadlift, Overhead Press, Power Clean
- Sets/Reps: 3×5 for most lifts; Deadlift: 1×5; Power Clean: 5×3
- Progression: Add ~5 lbs per session for upper body, ~10 lbs for squat/deadlift
- Weekly progression rate: ~7.5–15 lbs

**Philosophy:** Mastery of a small set of compound barbell movements. Maximizes novice adaptation by training whole body frequently.

**Pros:** Simple, highly effective for beginners, rapid strength gains
**Cons:** Limited exercise variety, becomes inadequate after a few months, high squat frequency can be fatiguing

---

### Program 2: StrongLifts 5×5

**Best For:** Beginners (similar to Starting Strength but with slight variations)

**Structure:**
- 3 days/week, alternating Workout A (Squat, Bench, Barbell Row) and Workout B (Squat, OHP, Deadlift)
- Sets/Reps: 5×5 (vs. Starting Strength's 3×5)
- Linear progression: add weight each session

**Pros:** Simple app support, beginner-friendly
**Cons:** Higher volume than Starting Strength can be harder to recover from; still limited variety

---

### Program 3: Jim Wendler 5/3/1

**Best For:** Intermediate lifters (6+ months experience); also has a beginner variant

**Structure:**
- 4-week cycles, 3–4 training days/week
- Four main lifts per cycle: Squat, Bench Press, Deadlift, Overhead Press
- Percentage-based loading off a "Training Max" (85–90% of true 1RM)

**Weekly Wave Structure:**
| Week | Sets x Reps | Intensities |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| 1    | 3×5         | 65%, 75%, 85% |
| 2    | 3×3         | 70%, 80%, 90% |
| 3    | 3×5/3/1     | 75%, 85%, 95% |
| 4    | Deload (3×5) | 40%, 50%, 60% |

- Last set each week is AMRAP (as many reps as possible)
- Progression: Add 5 lbs to upper body TM, 10 lbs to lower body TM after each 4-week cycle

**Accessory Work:** Various templates available (Beyond 5/3/1, BBB, FSL, etc.) adding volume via supplemental and accessory work

**5/3/1 for Beginners Variant:**
- 3 days/week with higher frequency; each day includes multiple main lifts

**Pros:** Sustainable long-term, flexible, extremely popular, well-documented
**Cons:** Slow progression by design; not optimal for pure beginners; requires patience

---

### Program 4: GZCL Method (Cody Lefever)

**Best For:** All levels — highly adaptable; beginner version is GZCLP

**Structure:** Tier-based system with three tiers of exercises:

| Tier | Type | Intensity | Volume | Example |
|------|------|-----------|--------|---------|
| T1 | Main competition lifts | >85% 1RM | 5×3, AMRAP last set | Squat, Bench, Deadlift |
| T2 | Secondary compound | 65–85% | 20–30 total reps (sets of 5–8) | SSB Squat, Pause Bench |
| T3 | Accessory/isolation | <65% | 30+ total reps (3×10+) | Leg Press, Tricep Pushdown |

**The 1:2:3 Rule:** For every rep in T1, do 2 in T2 and 3 in T3

**GZCLP (Beginner Version):**
- T1: 5×3 with last set AMRAP; progress when AMRAP ≥ 5 reps (add weight)
- T2: 3×10 linear progression
- Fail-state progressions built in

**Frequency:** 4 days/week typical (Upper/Lower or custom splits)

**Pros:** Extremely flexible, great for building volume intelligently, works for all levels
**Cons:** More complex to set up than Starting Strength or 5/3/1; can be confusing for beginners

---

### Program 5: nSuns 5/3/1 LP

**Best For:** Late-stage novice to early intermediate; those wanting high volume

**Structure:**
- Based on Wendler's 5/3/1 but with dramatically higher volume
- 4-day, 5-day, or 6-day variants
- Weekly AMRAP-based autoregulation: if you complete all reps at AMRAP, increase Training Max next week
- Main lift + competition lift pairing each day (e.g., Squat day also includes deadlift work)

**Volume per Session (example Squat day):**
- 9 total working sets on main lift (varying percentages)
- Plus secondary lift work

**Progression:**
- Increase TM by 5–10 lbs per week based on AMRAP performance
- If AMRAP < 1 rep: decrease TM by 10 lbs; 1–2 reps: no change; 3+ reps: increase

**Pros:** Very high volume → rapid strength and size gains; popular on Reddit; autoregulated
**Cons:** High fatigue accumulation; not for beginners; originally created anonymously (no coaching pedigree)

---

### Program 6: Sheiko

**Best For:** Intermediate to advanced powerlifters; technique-focused athletes

**Structure:**
- Developed by Russian coach Boris Sheiko
- 3–4 days/week
- High frequency on competition lifts: Bench 3×/week, Squat 2–3×/week, Deadlift 1–2×/week
- Submaximal intensities: 70–85% of 1RM for multiple sets
- Very high total volume

**Example 12-Week Structure:**
1. Preparatory Phase (weeks 1–4): High volume, moderate intensity
2. Intensification Phase (weeks 5–8): Reduced volume, higher intensity
3. Peaking Phase (weeks 9–12): Low volume, competition-level intensity

**Skill Classifications (Russian system):**
- Below Class III: Use beginner Sheiko
- Class II–I: Intermediate Sheiko
- CMS and above: Advanced Sheiko

**Pros:** Excellent for technical development, proven methodology, high-frequency practice
**Cons:** Very high volume—requires substantial recovery capacity; not appropriate for beginners or early intermediates

---

### Program 7: Conjugate / Westside Barbell Method (Louie Simmons)

**Best For:** Advanced powerlifters; equipped lifters; athletes with strong GPP base

**Structure:**
- 4 days/week: 2 Max Effort days + 2 Dynamic Effort days
- Upper/Lower split: ME Upper, ME Lower, DE Upper, DE Lower
- Rotate exercises every 1–3 weeks to prevent accommodation

**Max Effort (ME) Days:**
- Work up to a 1–3 rep max on a main lift variation (>90% intensity)
- Different exercise variation each week (e.g., box squat, floor press, rack pull)
- Followed by accessory work (repetition method): 2–4 sets × 6–10 reps

**Dynamic Effort (DE) Days:**
- 9 sets × 3 reps at 50–60% 1RM performed with maximum bar speed
- Focus on rate of force development (RFD)
- Often uses accommodating resistance (bands/chains)

**Pros:** Highly effective for advanced athletes; excellent for developing power and addressing weaknesses
**Cons:** Complex system; not appropriate for beginners/intermediates; heavily geared toward equipped powerlifting; requires knowledgeable coaching

---

### Program 8: Texas Method

**Best For:** Intermediate lifters transitioning out of linear progression

**Structure:**
- 3 days/week (Monday/Wednesday/Friday)
- Volume Day (Monday): 5×5 at ~90% of target Friday weight
- Recovery Day (Wednesday): 3×5 at ~80% (lighter, technique focus)
- Intensity Day (Friday): 1×5 at new PR weight

**Weekly progression:** Increase Friday intensity weight by 5 lbs/week

**Pros:** Great bridge from beginner LP to more complex intermediate programming; teaches weekly periodization
**Cons:** Can stall quickly if nutrition/sleep not optimal; less volume than some intermediates need

---

### Program 9: Madcow 5×5

**Best For:** Intermediate lifters; emphasis on size and strength simultaneously

**Structure:**
- 3 days/week, full-body each session
- Ramping sets of 5 (pyramid up to a heavy 5)
- Heavy, Light, Medium weekly structure
- Weekly progression (not every session like beginner LP)

**Progression:** Increase top set weight by ~2.5–5% per week

**Comparison with Texas Method:**
- Madcow: More hypertrophy focus, stricter template
- Texas Method: More explosive strength focus, more flexible as a template

---

| Program | Level | Frequency | Progression | Volume | Best For |

## TOPIC 2: NUTRITION FOR STRENGTH ATHLETES

### Caloric Needs

**TDEE Calculation:**
- BMR × Activity Multiplier = TDEE
- Activity Multipliers: Sedentary (×1.2), Lightly Active (×1.4), Moderately Active (×1.55), Very Active (×1.7), Extremely Active (×1.9)
- Powerlifters in hard training: typically 40–50 calories/kg body weight/day
- Example: 90 kg powerlifter = 3,600–4,500 calories/day

**Caloric Goals by Phase:**

| Phase | Surplus/Deficit | Rate of Change | Notes |
|-------|----------------|----------------|-------|
| Lean Bulk | +300–500 cal/day | 0.5–1% BW/month | Minimize fat gain |
| Aggressive Bulk | +500–750 cal/day | 1–1.5% BW/month | More fat accumulation |
| Cut | –300–500 cal/day | 0.5–1% BW/week | Preserve muscle |
| Recomp (maintenance) | ±0 cal | Very slow | Inefficient but works for beginners |
| Meet Peak | Varies | Weight class management | Adjust 4–8 weeks out |

**Key Principle:** Total calories and macros matter more than timing. Prioritize hitting daily targets before worrying about meal timing.

---

### Macronutrient Ratios for Powerlifters

**Protein:**
- Recommendation: 1.6–2.2 g/kg body weight/day (0.73–1.0 g/lb)
- Practical target: ~0.8–1.0 g/lb body weight
- ISSN recommendation: 1.4–2.0 g/kg/day
- Higher end (2.2 g/kg) during cutting phases to preserve muscle mass
- Per-meal target: 20–40g protein per meal to maximize muscle protein synthesis

**Carbohydrates:**
- Recommendation: 4–8 g/kg body weight/day (1.8–3.6 g/lb)
- Primary fuel source for high-intensity lifting (glycolytic energy system)
- Higher intake on heavy training days; can reduce on rest days
- Prioritize whole food sources: oats, rice, potatoes, fruits, bread

**Fats:**
- Recommendation: 20–35% of total calories
- Minimum ~0.5 g/kg/day to support hormone production (especially testosterone)
- Don't drop below 20% of calories — hormonal disruption risk
- Quality sources: olive oil, avocado, nuts, fatty fish, whole eggs

**Sample Macro Ratios:**

| Goal | Protein | Carbohydrates | Fat |
|------|---------|---------------|-----|
| Bulk | 30% | 45–50% | 20–25% |
| Cut | 35–40% | 35–40% | 20–25% |
| Maintenance | 30–35% | 40–45% | 20–25% |

---

### Meal Timing and Frequency

**General Principle:** Total daily intake matters most. Timing is secondary.

**Pre-Workout Nutrition:**
- Ideal timing: 2–4 hours before training
- Target: 20–30g protein + moderate carbohydrates (30–60g)
- If <1 hour before: liquid/blended meal (faster gastric emptying)
- Avoid very high fat meals immediately pre-workout (slows digestion)
- Caffeine: 30–60 minutes before training for peak effect

**Intra-Workout:**
- For sessions >90 minutes: carbohydrate drink (30–60g/hour) may help
- For typical 60-minute powerlifting sessions: not necessary
- Adequate pre-workout meal usually sufficient

**Post-Workout (Anabolic Window):**
- Traditional view: consume protein within 30–60 minutes
- Modern evidence: "anabolic window" is much wider — several hours
- Key finding: no difference in muscle growth when protein consumed pre- vs. post-workout
- Practical recommendation: eat a protein-rich meal within 2 hours of training
- Target: 20–40g protein post-workout

**Meal Frequency:**
- 3–6 meals/day all support muscle protein synthesis when total daily protein is met
- Distributing protein across 4–5 meals (each with 20–40g protein) may slightly optimize MPS
- Avoid large gaps (>5–6 hours) without protein during the day

---

### Best Foods for Powerlifters

**Protein Sources:**
- Chicken breast / turkey (lean, versatile, affordable)
- Lean ground beef, sirloin steak (creatine, iron, zinc)
- Eggs and egg whites (complete amino acid profile, affordable)
- Fish (salmon, tuna, tilapia) — salmon provides omega-3s too
- Greek yogurt, cottage cheese (casein protein, calcium)
- Whey/casein protein powders (convenient)
- Legumes (beans, lentils) — plant-based options

**Carbohydrate Sources:**
- White rice (easy to digest, quick energy)
- Oats (slow-digesting, good fiber)
- Sweet potatoes and white potatoes (micronutrient-dense)
- Fruit (bananas pre-workout for quick carbs)
- Whole grain bread and pasta
- Brown rice (fiber, micronutrients)

**Fat Sources:**
- Avocado (monounsaturated fats, potassium)
- Olive oil (anti-inflammatory)
- Nuts and nut butters (energy-dense)
- Fatty fish — salmon, mackerel, sardines (omega-3 EPA/DHA)
- Whole eggs (nutrient-dense, cholesterol supports testosterone)

**Micronutrient-Rich Foods:**
- Spinach, leafy greens (iron, magnesium, nitrates)
- Broccoli, colorful vegetables (antioxidants, vitamins)
- Berries (antioxidants, recovery support)
- Milk/dairy (calcium, vitamin D, leucine)

## TOPIC 3: SUPPLEMENTS WITH EVIDENCE

### Evidence Rating Scale
- **Strong:** Multiple RCTs, meta-analyses, consistent positive effects
- **Moderate:** Some RCTs with positive effects; mechanisms understood but variability exists
- **Weak:** Limited/mixed studies, theoretical benefits, or indirect evidence only

---

### Supplement 1: Creatine Monohydrate

**Evidence Level: STRONG (Tier 1)**

**Mechanism:** Increases phosphocreatine stores in muscle → faster ATP regeneration → more reps at high intensity → greater training volume over time

**Benefits:**
- Increases 1RM strength (well-established)
- Increases power output
- Improves repeated sprint/rep performance
- May aid recovery (recent 2026 study on short-term loading)
- May provide cognitive benefits (emerging evidence)

**Dosing Options:**

| Protocol | Dose | Duration | Notes |
|----------|------|----------|-------|
| Loading | 20–25 g/day (4–5 doses of 5g) | 5–7 days | Saturates muscles faster |
| Maintenance | 3–5 g/day | Ongoing | Continue after loading |
| No Loading | 3–5 g/day | Gradual — full saturation in 3–4 weeks | Same endpoint, slower |

- Loading dose can be calculated as: body weight (kg) × 0.3 = daily loading dose
- **Timing:** Does not matter significantly; consistency matters most
- **Form:** Monohydrate is gold standard; no evidence other forms (HCL, ethyl ester) are superior
- **Safety:** Extremely well-studied; safe for healthy individuals; stay well hydrated

**Practical Note:** Most cost-effective performance supplement available. No need for expensive forms.

---

### Supplement 2: Protein Supplements (Whey, Casein, Plant-Based)

**Evidence Level: STRONG (Tier 1) for protein adequacy; MODERATE for specific forms**

**Whey Protein:**
- Rapidly absorbed (fast-digesting)
- Complete amino acid profile; high leucine content (key trigger for MPS)
- Ideal: post-workout or any time additional protein is needed
- Evidence: Consistently superior to plant proteins in head-to-head comparisons for muscle growth
- Typical dose: 20–40g per serving

**Casein Protein:**
- Slowly absorbed (6–8 hours digestion)
- Ideal: before bed for overnight muscle protein synthesis
- Reduces muscle protein breakdown during fasting periods
- Good choice: cottage cheese, Greek yogurt as whole food sources
- Typical dose: 30–40g before bed

**Plant-Based Proteins (Pea, Rice, Soy):**
- Soy protein: complete amino acids; evidence shows similar results to whey in some studies
- Pea + rice protein blend: addresses incomplete amino acid profiles of individual plant proteins
- Generally lower leucine content → may require higher doses to match whey's effect
- 2024 meta-analysis: whey significantly outperforms soy for muscle growth
- Some studies show no difference when total protein is matched
- Recommendation: Plant-based athletes should consume slightly higher total protein (~10–15% more) to compensate

**Key Principle:** Food-based protein sources are equally effective. Supplements are for convenience when whole foods are insufficient.

---

### Supplement 3: Beta-Alanine

**Evidence Level: MODERATE (Tier 2)**

**Mechanism:** Increases muscle carnosine content → buffers lactic acid → delays fatigue during high-rep/high-intensity efforts

**Benefits:**
- Increases carnosine by up to 64% after 4 weeks at 4–6 g/day
- Most effective for high-rep work (8–15+ reps) or repeated bouts
- 5-week study: improved 1RM, power output, total sets completed
- Less direct benefit for pure 1RM strength (low-rep work is not limited by lactate)

**Dosing:**
- 3.2–6.4 g/day, split into 2–4 doses
- Loading phase: 4–6 g/day for 4–6 weeks
- Maintenance: 1.2–2.4 g/day once muscle carnosine is elevated

**Side Effect:** Paresthesia (skin tingling) — harmless; reduced by splitting doses or using sustained-release formula

**Applicability to Powerlifting:** Moderate — more relevant for higher-rep training blocks or conditioning work than pure low-rep strength training

---

### Supplement 4: Caffeine

**Evidence Level: STRONG (Tier 1)**

**Mechanism:** Adenosine receptor antagonist → increased alertness, reduced perceived effort, improved neuromuscular function

**Benefits:**
- Consistently improves power output
- Improves strength (particularly when fatigued)
- Enhanced focus and motivation for training
- Performance benefits at 3–6 mg/kg body weight

**Dosing:**
- Effective range: 3–6 mg/kg body weight (e.g., 200–400 mg for most adults)
- Timing: 30–60 minutes before training
- Tolerance develops with daily use; cycle off periodically (1–2 weeks off) to restore sensitivity

**Considerations:**
- Avoid within 6 hours of sleep (half-life ~5–6 hours)
- May cause anxiety/jitteriness at high doses
- Combined with creatine: no negative interaction; potentially additive

**Sources:** Coffee, pre-workout supplements, caffeine pills

---

### Supplement 5: Citrulline Malate

**Evidence Level: MODERATE (Tier 2)**

**Mechanism:** Increases arginine availability → increased nitric oxide → improved blood flow; also supports ammonia clearance reducing fatigue

**Benefits:**
- Significant improvement in training volume (52.9% more reps in one well-cited study)
- 40% reduction in muscle soreness at 24–48 hours post-training
- May improve aerobic performance
- Supports recovery between sets

**Dosing:**
- 6–8 g of citrulline malate (2:1 ratio) or 3–6 g L-citrulline, taken 60 min before training
- The placebo-controlled study used 8 g citrulline malate

**Considerations:**
- More relevant for higher-volume training than pure strength work
- Well-tolerated; no major side effects reported
- Often found in pre-workout supplements

---

### Supplement 6: Vitamin D

**Evidence Level: MODERATE (Tier 2) — higher if deficient**

**Mechanism:** Steroid hormone precursor; affects gene expression related to muscle function, immune health, bone density, testosterone production

**Benefits:**
- Supports bone health (calcium absorption, reduces stress fracture risk)
- Muscle function (deficiency causes weakness/injury)
- Immune function (reduces illness-related training disruptions)
- Testosterone production support
- Athletes are commonly deficient, especially those training indoors

**Dosing:**
- General: 1,000–2,000 IU/day for maintenance
- Deficient athletes: 2,000–5,000 IU/day (confirm with blood test)
- Optimal blood level: 40–60 ng/mL (100–150 nmol/L)
- Take with a fat-containing meal (fat-soluble vitamin)

**Considerations:** High prevalence of deficiency in athletes; testing recommended before high-dose supplementation; toxicity possible at very high doses (>10,000 IU/day chronic)

---

### Supplement 7: Fish Oil (Omega-3 Fatty Acids — EPA/DHA)

**Evidence Level: MODERATE (Tier 2)**

**Mechanism:** Anti-inflammatory eicosanoid production; precursor to prostaglandins; membrane phospholipid incorporation

**Benefits:**
- Reduces post-exercise inflammation (decreased IL-6, TNF-α, CK)
- Reduces DOMS (delayed onset muscle soreness)
- May modestly improve strength gains (2g/day for 13 weeks showed greater gains vs. training alone)
- Supports cardiovascular health
- Joint health support relevant for heavy lifting

**Dosing:**
- Effective range: 2,000–4,000 mg/day of combined EPA+DHA
- For athletic performance/recovery: at least 2,400 mg/day for minimum 4.5 weeks
- Elite athletes: up to 4 g/day for maximum inflammation control

**Considerations:**
- Take with meals to reduce fish burps
- Evidence stronger for recovery than direct strength performance
- Whole food source: fatty fish (salmon, mackerel, sardines) 2–3×/week

---

### Supplement 8: Magnesium

**Evidence Level: MODERATE (Tier 2) — higher if deficient**

**Mechanism:** Involved in 300+ enzymatic reactions; ATP production, muscle contraction, electrolyte balance, protein synthesis, testosterone regulation

**Benefits:**
- Muscle function and contraction
- Improves sleep quality (especially magnesium glycinate)
- Supports testosterone levels
- Reduces muscle cramps
- Most athletes fail to meet recommended intake through diet alone

**Dosing:**
- 300–400 mg/day elemental magnesium
- Preferred forms: Magnesium glycinate (best absorbed, least GI upset), Magnesium citrate
- Avoid: Magnesium oxide (poor absorption)
- Timing: Before bed (enhances sleep quality)

---

### Supplement 9: Zinc

**Evidence Level: MODERATE (Tier 2) — higher if deficient**

**Mechanism:** Involved in testosterone synthesis, immune function, protein synthesis, enzyme function

**Benefits:**
- Supports testosterone production (especially when depleted by sweating)
- Immune system support
- Recovery support

**Dosing:**
- 25–45 mg/day of elemental zinc
- Often combined with magnesium (ZMA — Zinc, Magnesium, B6)
- Note: ZMA studies have mixed results; direct zinc supplementation for deficiency correction has clearer benefit
- Caution: High-dose zinc (>40mg/day chronic) can interfere with copper absorption

---

| Supplement | Evidence Level | Primary Benefit | Dose | Priority |
|------------|---------------|-----------------|------|----------|
| Creatine Monohydrate | **STRONG** | Strength, power, muscle | 3–5 g/day | #1 — Buy this first |
| Caffeine | **STRONG** | Performance, focus | 3–6 mg/kg | #2 — Already in coffee |
| Whey/Casein Protein | **STRONG** | Muscle protein synthesis | 20–40 g/serving | #3 if diet protein is low |
| Beta-Alanine | **MODERATE** | Buffering, volume | 3.2–6.4 g/day | #4 — More for higher reps |
| Citrulline Malate | **MODERATE** | Volume, recovery | 6–8 g pre-workout | #5 — Training volume focus |
