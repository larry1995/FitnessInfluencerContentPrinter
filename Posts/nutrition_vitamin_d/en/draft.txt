VITAMIN D — THE SUNSHINE SUPPLEMENT EVERY LIFTER NEEDS TO CHECK

Up to 36% of elite athletes are vitamin D deficient. And if you train indoors, your risk is even higher. Here's why it matters:

1. DEFICIENCY SHRINKS YOUR MUSCLE FIBERS
Vitamin D deficiency causes proximal muscle weakness and a measurable reduction in Type II (fast-twitch) muscle fiber diameter. These are the exact fibers responsible for explosive strength and power.

2. YOUR INJURY RISK GOES UP
Athletes with low vitamin D had a 1.5-2x higher rate of stress fractures and musculoskeletal injuries. A study of NFL players found 26% were deficient — and those players had significantly more muscle injuries.

3. CORRECTING DEFICIENCY IMPROVES STRENGTH
A 2024 meta-analysis found that vitamin D3 supplementation significantly improved lower extremity strength in athletes who started with deficient levels. The key word: DEFICIENT. If your levels are already adequate, extra vitamin D won't add more gains.

4. THE THRESHOLD EFFECT
Once your serum 25(OH)D reaches 75 nmol/L (30 ng/mL), additional supplementation does NOT further enhance maximal strength or power. More isn't better — adequate is the goal.

5. MOST PEOPLE DON'T GET ENOUGH
If you live above 37 degrees latitude (that includes most of the US north of Santa Cruz), train primarily indoors, or have darker skin, you're at higher risk for deficiency. Sun exposure alone often isn't enough.

HOW TO OPTIMIZE:
- GET TESTED: Ask your doctor for a 25(OH)D blood test
- TARGET LEVEL: 40-60 ng/mL (100-150 nmol/L)
- SUPPLEMENT DOSE: 2,000-5,000 IU vitamin D3 daily (adjust based on blood levels)
- TAKE WITH FAT: Vitamin D is fat-soluble — take it with a meal
- FOOD SOURCES: Fatty fish, egg yolks, fortified dairy (but hard to get enough from food alone)
- RETEST: Check levels after 8-12 weeks of supplementation

WHO SHOULD DEFINITELY GET TESTED:
- Indoor athletes and gym-goers
- People living in northern latitudes
- Those with darker skin tones
- Anyone training through winter months
- Athletes with recurring injuries or slow recovery

THE BOTTOM LINE:
You don't need megadoses. You need to know your level and fix it if it's low. A simple blood test and a $10 bottle of D3 could be the easiest performance upgrade you make this year.
