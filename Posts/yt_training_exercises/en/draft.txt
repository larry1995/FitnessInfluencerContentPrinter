7 UNDERRATED EXERCISES YOU SHOULD BE DOING

Jeff Nippard breaks down 7 movements with real scientific support that most lifters skip. Every one of these targets common weak points.

1. DEAD HANGS
Opens the shoulder joint, decompresses the spine, builds forearm and grip strength, and strengthens the core. Start with 30-second holds and work up to 60+ seconds. Especially valuable after heavy squats or deadlifts.

2. INCLINE DUMBBELL CURLS
The stretched position at the bottom creates a stronger hypertrophy signal. Research shows exercises that load the muscle at long muscle lengths produce more growth than those that load at short lengths.

3. DRAGON FLAGS
One of the most challenging core exercises. Targets the rectus abdominis through a massive range of motion while your obliques and serratus muscles stabilize. Regress to tucked knees if you can't do a full rep.

4. HEEL-ELEVATED GOBLET SQUATS
Elevating your heels on a plate or wedge lets your knees travel forward, dramatically increasing quad emphasis. Research shows deeper knee flexion targets the vastus medialis and lateralis more effectively. Use a dumbbell or kettlebell and control the tempo.

5. CABLE LATERAL RAISES (BEHIND THE BODY)
Running the cable behind your body shifts peak tension to where the side delts are most stretched. This is arguably the best variation for building the wide, tapered look that separates good physiques from great ones.

WHAT THESE EXERCISES HAVE IN COMMON:
- They target muscles at LONG lengths (stretch-mediated hypertrophy)
- They fill gaps that standard programs miss (rotator cuff, serratus, forearms)
- They're joint-friendly when done correctly
- They transfer to better performance on your main lifts

HOW TO ADD THEM:
- Dead hangs: between warm-up sets, 2-3 x 30-60 sec
- Incline curls: replace one standard curl variation
- Dragon flags: end of core work, 3 x 6-10
- Smith squats: after barbell squats on high-volume days
- Cable lateral raises: 3 x 12-15, behind-body setup

THE BOTTOM LINE:
You don't need exotic exercises. But strategically adding underused movements can fill weak points that heavy compounds alone won't fix.
