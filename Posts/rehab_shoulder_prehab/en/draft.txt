🏋️ BULLETPROOF YOUR SHOULDERS — 5 PREHAB EXERCISES EVERY LIFTER NEEDS

Shoulder injuries are the #1 overuse injury in resistance training. A study of recreational lifters found that 36% reported current or recent shoulder pain — most from bench press and overhead work.

The fix isn't avoiding pressing. It's building the stabilizers that protect your shoulders BEFORE they break.

HERE ARE 5 EXERCISES BACKED BY EMG AND CLINICAL RESEARCH:

1️⃣ BAND PULL-APARTS
Target: Rear delts, middle/lower trapezius, rhomboids
Why: These muscles counterbalance the heavy pressing that dominates most programs. Weakness here causes the shoulder to roll forward, creating impingement.
How: Hold a light resistance band at arm's length. Pull it apart until it touches your chest. Squeeze your shoulder blades together. 3 x 15-20 reps.
When: Between every pressing warm-up set. Seriously — every single one.

2️⃣ FACE PULLS WITH EXTERNAL ROTATION
Target: Rear delts, infraspinatus, teres minor, lower traps
Why: A 2009 EMG study by Reinold et al. found that exercises combining pulling with external rotation produced the highest combined activation of the rotator cuff and scapular stabilizers among common shoulder rehabilitation exercises.
How: Cable at face height. Pull to your forehead, then rotate hands outward so your arms form a "double bicep" pose. 3 x 15-20.
Key cue: Initiate with your rear delts, not your biceps. The rotation at the end is the most important part.

3️⃣ SIDE-LYING EXTERNAL ROTATION
Target: Infraspinatus, teres minor (the two rotator cuff muscles most responsible for shoulder stability during pressing)
Why: Isolation of the external rotators is critical for lifters whose internal rotators (pecs, lats, front delts) are significantly stronger — which is almost every powerlifter.
How: Lie on your side, upper arm against your ribs, elbow at 90 degrees. Rotate a light dumbbell (3-8 lbs) upward. Slow and controlled. 3 x 12-15 each side.
Key cue: Keep your elbow pinned to your side. If it lifts off, the weight is too heavy.

4️⃣ SCAPULAR WALL SLIDES
Target: Lower trapezius, serratus anterior
Why: These muscles control scapular upward rotation — the movement your shoulder blade MUST make during overhead pressing. Poor scapular control = impingement under load.
How: Stand with back, head, and arms against a wall. Slide arms up overhead (maintaining wall contact) and back down. 3 x 10-12.
Key cue: If your lower back arches off the wall, you don't have the mobility yet — go only as high as you can while staying flat.

5️⃣ PRONE Y-T-W RAISES
Target: Lower traps (Y), middle traps (T), rotator cuff (W)
Why: These three positions collectively activate the entire posterior shoulder girdle. A 2015 systematic review identified these movements as among the most effective for scapular muscle activation.
How: Lie face down on an incline bench (30-45 degrees). With light dumbbells (2-5 lbs) or no weight:
Y — Arms extended overhead at 45 degrees, thumbs up. 8-10 reps.
T — Arms straight out to sides, thumbs up. 8-10 reps.
W — Elbows bent, squeeze shoulder blades, externally rotate. 8-10 reps.
1-2 rounds through all three positions.

THE PREHAB ROUTINE (10 MINUTES, 3x/WEEK):
1. Band Pull-Aparts: 2 x 20
2. Face Pulls w/ External Rotation: 2 x 15
3. Side-Lying External Rotation: 2 x 12 each side
4. Scapular Wall Slides: 2 x 10
5. Prone Y-T-W: 1 round through all three

WHEN TO DO IT:
- Before pressing sessions (as part of warm-up)
- On off days (as recovery work)
- After pressing sessions (as cooldown)
- Any time your shoulders feel "crunchy" or tight

THE BOTTOM LINE:
You wouldn't squat heavy without warming up your hips. Stop pressing heavy without preparing your shoulders. 10 minutes of prehab, 3 times per week, can prevent the shoulder injury that takes you out for months. Prevention is always cheaper than rehab.
