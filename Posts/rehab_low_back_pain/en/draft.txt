🏋️ LOW BACK PAIN? STOP RESTING AND START MOVING.

"My back hurts so I stopped lifting."

This is one of the most common — and most counterproductive — responses to low back pain. The research is overwhelming: for non-specific low back pain, movement is medicine. Rest makes it worse.

WHAT THE SCIENCE SAYS:

A 2021 Cochrane systematic review of 249 studies (24,486 participants) found that exercise therapy is more effective than rest, medication, or manual therapy alone for chronic non-specific low back pain. Exercise reduced pain intensity AND improved function — with benefits lasting beyond the treatment period.

Even more compelling: a 2025 meta-analysis of 10 RCTs (434 participants) found that resistance training specifically produced significant improvements in pain intensity (SMD = -1.15), quality of life (SMD = 0.82), and disability (SMD = -2.76) for chronic low back pain. Lifting doesn't just "not hurt" your back — it TREATS back pain.

HERE ARE 5 EVIDENCE-BASED EXERCISES:

1️⃣ BIRD-DOGS (McGill's Big 3)
Why: Trains spinal stability through anti-rotation and anti-extension without loading the spine
How: Hands and knees position. Extend opposite arm and leg simultaneously. Hold 8-10 seconds. 3 x 6 each side.
Key cue: Keep your back FLAT — if a glass of water would spill off your lower back, you're moving too much.

2️⃣ McGILL CURL-UPS (McGill's Big 3)
Why: Builds anterior core endurance without spinal flexion (unlike crunches, which can aggravate disc issues)
How: Lie on back, one knee bent, one leg straight. Hands under lower back to maintain neutral spine. Lift head and shoulders 1-2 inches. Hold 8-10 seconds. 3 x 6.
Key cue: Your lower back should NOT flatten against the floor. Maintain the natural arch.

3️⃣ SIDE PLANKS (McGill's Big 3)
Why: Lateral core stability is often the weakest link. The quadratus lumborum and obliques are critical spinal stabilizers.
How: Elbow under shoulder, hips stacked. Lift hips off ground. Hold 8-10 seconds. 3 x 4-6 each side. Regress to knees if needed.
Key cue: Straight line from head to feet. Don't let hips sag or pike up.

4️⃣ HIP HINGES (BODYWEIGHT TO LOADED)
Why: Most back pain in lifters comes from poor hip hinge mechanics — they flex their spine when they should be hinging at their hips.
How: Stand with feet hip-width. Push hips BACK while keeping a neutral spine. Feel the stretch in hamstrings. Return by squeezing glutes. Progress: bodyweight → dowel → kettlebell → barbell RDL.
Reps: 3 x 10, focus on pattern quality over load.
Key cue: The bar (or hands) should stay close to your body. This is NOT a squat.

5️⃣ LOADED CARRIES (FARMER'S WALKS)
Why: Builds whole-body spinal stability under load. Research shows loaded carries activate the erector spinae, obliques, and deep stabilizers more effectively than isolated core exercises.
How: Pick up heavy dumbbells or farmer's walk handles. Walk with tall posture, shoulders back, core braced. 3 x 40 meters.
Key cue: Walk like you're trying to NOT look like you're carrying heavy things. Tall, smooth, no leaning.

WHEN TO USE THESE:
- As a daily "back health" routine (10-15 minutes)
- As a warm-up before squat or deadlift sessions
- During a flare-up: start with McGill's Big 3 only, pain-free range
- As you improve: progress to loaded carries and hip hinges

WHEN TO SEE A PROFESSIONAL:
- Pain radiating below the knee (sciatica pattern)
- Numbness or tingling in legs or feet
- Loss of bladder or bowel control (emergency)
- Pain that doesn't improve after 4-6 weeks of consistent exercise
- Pain that worsens significantly during or after these exercises

THE BOTTOM LINE:
Most low back pain in lifters resolves with better movement, not less movement. The McGill Big 3 + hip hinges + loaded carries build the stability your spine needs to handle heavy training. Start with bodyweight, progress slowly, and don't skip these on good days.
