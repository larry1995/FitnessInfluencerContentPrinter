🏋️ SHOULD YOU TRAIN PAST FAILURE? (New Research)

Dr. Mike Israetel breaks down a new study on training beyond failure — and the results might surprise you.

HERE'S WHAT THE STUDY FOUND:

Training past failure using techniques like forced reps, drop sets, and rest-pause sets CAN produce additional muscle growth compared to stopping at failure.

But there's a catch. Here's the full picture:

✅ WHEN IT WORKS:
• When total volume is matched, past-failure techniques produced slightly more hypertrophy
• Mechanical tension stays high even as reps get sloppy
• Best applied to isolation exercises and machine work
• Most effective for advanced lifters who need the extra stimulus

⚠️ WHEN IT BACKFIRES:
• Massively increases fatigue and recovery demands
• Diminishing returns — the extra growth is small relative to the extra effort
• High injury risk if applied to heavy compound lifts
• Can crush your ability to train hard in subsequent sessions

THE PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY:
Use past-failure techniques sparingly. Save them for the last set of an isolation exercise — not your heavy squats and deadlifts. 1-2 sets per session is plenty.

Think of it as a spice, not the main dish.
