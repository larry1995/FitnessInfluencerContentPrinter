🏋️ SHOULDER PAIN ON BENCH? DON'T STOP — MODIFY.

Too many lifters quit benching when their shoulder flares up. You don't have to. You just have to be smart about it.

Juggernaut Training Systems lays out how to keep benching while your shoulder heals:

1️⃣ NARROW YOUR GRIP
Wider grip = more shoulder stress. Bring your hands in 1-2 finger widths. You'll lose some chest activation but save your shoulders.

2️⃣ USE A SLIGHT DECLINE
15-20° decline shifts the load away from the front delt. Many lifters find this completely pain-free even when flat bench hurts.

3️⃣ SLOW DOWN THE ECCENTRIC
3-4 second lowering phase reduces the shock at the bottom. Controlled reps = less impingement.

4️⃣ FLOOR PRESS
Limits range of motion at the bottom — exactly where most shoulder pain happens. Great for maintaining pressing strength.

5️⃣ SWAP TO DUMBBELLS
Dumbbells let your shoulders find their natural path. Fixed barbells force your joints into one groove.

⚠️ WHAT TO AVOID:
• Flat barbell bench with a wide grip
• Behind-the-neck press (always)
• Dips with excessive depth
• Pushing through sharp pain (dull ache = usually OK, sharp = stop)

💡 Pain is information, not a stop sign. Work around it, not through it.
