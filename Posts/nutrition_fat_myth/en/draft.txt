"EATING FAT MAKES YOU FAT" — THE MYTH THAT WON'T DIE

This might be the most damaging nutrition myth in fitness history. And the science has been clear for over a decade: dietary fat does NOT make you fat. Here's the evidence.

1. CALORIC SURPLUS MAKES YOU FAT — NOT FAT ITSELF
A landmark metabolic ward study by Hall et al. (2015) — the most tightly controlled diet study ever conducted — found that when calories were equated, reducing dietary fat caused MORE body fat loss than reducing carbs by the same amount. But here's the kicker: the difference was tiny (463g over 6 days). What mattered was total calories, not the macronutrient source.

2. LOW-FAT DIETS DON'T OUTPERFORM HIGH-FAT DIETS
A 2020 network meta-analysis of 121 diet trials found NO significant difference in weight loss between low-fat and higher-fat diets when calories and protein were matched. The diet you can stick to wins — regardless of fat content.

3. DIETARY FAT IS ESSENTIAL FOR HORMONE PRODUCTION
Testosterone production requires cholesterol and dietary fat. A 2021 meta-analysis found that men on very low-fat diets (<20% of calories from fat) had significantly lower testosterone levels compared to moderate-fat diets (30-40%). For powerlifters, this matters.

4. FAT KEEPS YOU FULL
Fat is the most satiating macronutrient per calorie when combined with protein and fiber. Removing fat from your diet often leads to increased hunger and worse diet adherence — the exact opposite of what you want for body composition.

5. YOUR BRAIN NEEDS IT
60% of your brain is fat. Essential fatty acids (omega-3, omega-6) are required for cell membrane integrity, inflammation regulation, and cognitive function. Chronically low fat intake is associated with impaired mood and cognitive performance.

PRACTICAL GUIDELINES FOR LIFTERS:
- Aim for 25-35% of total calories from fat (minimum 0.3g per lb bodyweight)
- Prioritize unsaturated sources: olive oil, avocado, nuts, fatty fish
- Don't fear saturated fat — it's fine in moderation. The dose makes the poison.
- On a cut, fat can drop to 20% of calories temporarily, but don't go lower
- Track total calories and protein first — fat and carbs are flexible

WHAT ACTUALLY MATTERS FOR BODY COMPOSITION:
1. Total caloric intake (surplus = gain, deficit = lose)
2. Protein intake (0.7-1.0g/lb for lifters)
3. Resistance training (the real driver of body recomposition)
4. Sleep and recovery
5. Adherence over time (months, not days)

THE BOTTOM LINE:
Fat doesn't make you fat. Excess calories make you fat. Eat your avocados, cook with olive oil, and stop being afraid of egg yolks. Your hormones and your taste buds will thank you.
