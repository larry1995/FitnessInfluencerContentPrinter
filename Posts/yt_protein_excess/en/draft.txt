"YOU CAN ONLY ABSORB 30g OF PROTEIN PER MEAL" — TRUE OR FALSE?

FALSE. This myth needs to die. Here's what the research actually shows.

1. YOUR BODY ABSORBS ALL OF IT
When you eat a large dose of protein, your gut slows digestion to absorb everything. A 2024 study showed muscle protein synthesis remains elevated 5+ hours after a large protein meal. Nothing is "wasted."

2. MORE PROTEIN = MORE SYNTHESIS
A study comparing 40g vs 70g of protein post-workout found the 70g group had significantly MORE muscle protein synthesis. The old 30g ceiling has been repeatedly disproven.

3. YOUR GUT ADAPTS
Research shows your digestive system adapts to your eating patterns. If you routinely eat large meals, your body becomes more efficient at extracting amino acids from them.

4. DISTRIBUTION STILL MATTERS (A LITTLE)
A 2024 randomized trial found that spreading protein evenly across 4 meals increased weekly muscle protein synthesis by ~12% compared to eating most protein in one meal. So distribution helps — but it's not required.

5. THE LEUCINE TRIGGER
Each meal needs approximately 2.5g of leucine to maximally stimulate muscle protein synthesis. A 40-50g protein shake easily hits this threshold. A 20g shake is borderline.

PRACTICAL TAKEAWAYS:
- Total daily protein is king: 0.7-1.0g per lb bodyweight
- 3-5 protein feedings per day is optimal but not mandatory
- A big post-workout shake (40-50g) is perfectly fine
- If you eat 2-3 meals per day, larger servings work just as well
- Don't stress about per-meal protein limits

THE BOTTOM LINE:
Eat enough protein daily. Don't overthink meal timing. Your body is smarter than Instagram broscience suggests.
